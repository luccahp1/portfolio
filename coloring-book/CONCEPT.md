# The Wander Balloon
### A coloring adventure with Sorrel & Mo — 24 original interior pages

## A note on the source listing

The Amazon listing supplied with the brief (`a.co/d/08AyKVMQ`) could not be
opened from this environment — outbound requests to `a.co` are blocked by the
network egress proxy, so no title, cover, artwork or description was ever
retrieved or seen.

Everything below was therefore built for the **default mainstream kids'
coloring-book audience**: **ages 4–8**, gift/activity buyer, 8.5 × 11 in, single
subject per page, bold outlines. That is the segment nearly all such listings
sit in. If the real listing is aimed somewhere else — toddler (2–4, far fewer
lines), tween (9–12, dense detail), or a specific theme like dinosaurs,
unicorns, trucks or ocean — say so and the same system re-skins to it: the cast,
the page list and the line weights are all parameters of one style engine, not
24 hand-tuned one-offs.

**Originality:** nothing here is traced, adapted or referenced from any existing
book, character or artwork. Every shape on every page is generated from
primitives in `src/inkstyle.py`. No trademarked or copyrighted characters, no
brand marks, no likenesses, no text anywhere inside the book.

## The concept

A patchwork hot-air balloon called **the Wander** carries two small friends on a
round trip out of their meadow and home again. Every page is one stop on that
journey, so the book has an arc a child can follow from cover to cover — but any
page still works completely on its own, pulled out and colored in isolation.

Why this concept holds up over 24 pages:

* **Two recurring characters** give a child someone to look for on every page —
  the single biggest driver of "let's do another one."
* **One journey, many biomes** — meadow, mountain, forest, jungle, reef, desert,
  night sky — so the subject matter never repeats while the style never changes.
* **The balloon itself is a coloring toy.** Three big panels carry three
  different patterns (dots, chevrons, stars), so the same object rewards a
  different choice every time it appears.
* **Difficulty ramps** from a single large subject on page 1 to a full scene on
  page 24, without any page ever getting fiddly.

### The cast

| Who | What they look like | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Sorrel** | A round-cheeked fox cub in a striped scarf, with flight goggles pushed up on their forehead | Big simple head shapes, four bold ear/muzzle landmarks — recognizable at any size, easy for a small hand to color inside |
| **Mo** | A calm snail with a spiral shell, riding along as navigator | The spiral is a natural "follow the line" invitation, and the shell is a big empty canvas |
| **The Wander** | A patchwork balloon: three panels, three patterns, a woven basket | The book's hero object and its cover anchor |

Supporting animals (mountain goats, a hedgehog with a berry cart, a whale, a
jerboa) appear once each. They're all drawn with the same face module — two
round eyes, one smile curve — so the whole book reads as one hand.

deliberately the two easiest in the book — the first page a child opens should
never be the hardest.

## Cover direction

The interiors are pure line art. The cover is the only place color, type and
texture appear.

**Front cover.** The Wander on a rising diagonal from lower-left to upper-right,
filling about 60% of the cover. Sorrel leans over the basket rim, one paw up,
scarf streaming back along the diagonal; Mo rides the rim with the map. Three
cloud-sheep and a small arc of birds sit behind at low contrast. The balloon's
three panels are the color story: a warm coral panel, a butter-yellow panel and
a deep teal star panel, all over a sky that runs from pale mint at the horizon
to a deeper teal at the top edge. A thin cream keyline frames the whole cover
about 0.25 in inside the trim, so the art reads as a poster rather than a
bleed-off photo — this also survives thumbnail size, which is where the buying
decision is actually made.

**Type.** Title in a rounded, slightly bouncy hand-lettered style, arched over
the balloon crown, cream with a soft coral drop-shade — set in an open-licence
face (Baloo 2, Fredoka or Quicksand under the SIL OFL) or hand-drawn, never a
licensed novelty font. Subtitle on one line beneath the balloon basket. A small
cream banner in the lower-right corner carries the page-count callout. No
character on the cover ever overlaps the title.

**Back cover.** A tiled pattern of tiny outline balloons in a lighter tint of
the sky color; a centered cream card holding a three-line blurb; one small
uncolored interior sample (page 6, the kites) printed as line art to show
buyers exactly what's inside; the age range in the bottom corner.

**Spine.** At 48 leaves on white paper the spine measures roughly 0.11 in —
below the threshold where spine text is legal or legible on most POD services.
Keep the spine a flat color field, no type.

**Palette (cover only):** teal `#1F7A8C`, mint `#BFDBD3`, coral `#F0705A`,
butter `#F6C567`, cream `#FBF6EC`, ink `#1A1A1A`.

## Print specification

| | |
|---|---|
| Trim | 8.5 × 11 in portrait |
| Interior | 24 art pages, black line art on white, no bleed |
| Recommended binding | 48 pages — art on odd pages, blanks on even, so markers can't bleed onto another drawing |
| Safe margin | 1.0 in on all four sides of every page (art never crosses it) |
| Line weights | 2.8 mm outer silhouettes / 2.0 mm secondary / 1.5 mm detail |
| Minimum colorable gap | ~5 mm, so a fat crayon still lands inside every shape |
| Ink | 100% K only. No gray, no halftone, no screens, no gradients |
| Source files | Vector SVG (`pages/`), print PDF (`print/`) — scales to any trim without redrawing |

Every page is a closed-shape drawing: no open contours for color to leak out of,
nothing smaller than a crayon tip, and no text anywhere inside the book, which
also means the interiors need no translation to sell in another language.
